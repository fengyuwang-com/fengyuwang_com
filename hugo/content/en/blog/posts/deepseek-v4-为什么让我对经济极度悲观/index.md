Final video outline: DeepSeek V4 — why I am extremely pessimistic about the future economy (the brutal reality version for ordinary people)

Core thesis (the soul of the whole piece, final version)

Every past wave of technological progress made people more valuable, created new jobs, and gave ordinary people a ladder up.
But this round of AI extreme commoditization — DeepSeek V4 — flattens basic mental labor outright, breaks through the wage floor, and takes away ordinary people's livelihoods.

My pessimism is not about failing to keep up with the times.
It is that this time, technological progress no longer has room for ordinary workers.


0-25s Opening hook (sharp contrast, personal lived experience)

The entire internet is celebrating in unison:
DeepSeek V4's performance is explosive, a domestic breakthrough, crushing the overseas models, god-tier value for money, AI empowering everyone.

But I am an ordinary person with a master's degree, years of marketing-planning experience, and currently genuinely unemployed.
I have to say the one true thing nobody wants to hear:
The stronger and cheaper V4 gets, the more despairing I become about the future economy.

This round of technological dividend belongs to capital, to compute, to a small elite.
For the vast majority of ordinary workers, this is a structural massacre of employment.


25-75s First layer of pessimism: the AI industry has completely lost any healthy business logic

Every mainstream model worldwide — GPT included — is raising prices, capping usage, protecting margins.
Only DeepSeek V4 in China is racing in the opposite direction:
performance doubled, price smashed straight through the floor.

With the official pricing:

- GPT-4-class traditional compute: a dozen-plus RMB / million tokens
- DeepSeek V4 with KV cache hits: 0.025 RMB / million tokens

The overall price gap is more than 15x.

That produces a deeply deformed industry ending:
the technology keeps getting infinitely stronger, but the industry never makes money.

Every AI company can only burn cash, race to the bottom, slash prices.
No profit → no new high-paying jobs → the only moves left for companies: cut costs, cut headcount, replace humans with machines.

The AI industry itself has already lost the virtuous cycle of driving employment and driving the economy.


75-150s Second layer, the core blow: this is not empowerment, it is absolute replacement on the cost dimension (the hardest-hitting part of the piece)

Everyone online keeps laundering the same line: AI is just a tool, it empowers humans, it won't replace them.

Today I'll run a real, brutal cost calculation from an employer's point of view, and everyone will understand why I'm unemployed and why I'm pessimistic:

1) Human cost

An ordinary marketing planner, copywriter, entry-level white-collar worker
monthly salary 6k-10k RMB
real daily cost to the employer (salary + social insurance + workstation + management): 300-500 RMB/day
Gets tired, takes breaks, makes mistakes, needs training, needs emotional support, needs promotions.

2) DeepSeek V4 cost (real enterprise deployment price)

Marketing plans, proposals, copywriting, retrospectives, organizing slide decks, batch content production —
these are ultra-high-reuse scenarios, with KV cache hit rates of 95%-99%.

In these scenarios:
a company running full intensity all day, hundreds of thousands to a million tokens
daily compute cost: 5-10 RMB at most.

The final brutal comparison

- A human: 300-500 RMB/day, with a ceiling, with costs, with emotions
- AI: 10 RMB/day, no days off all year, zero mistakes, no social insurance, no management, unlimited mass production

So let me ask:
If you were the boss, why would you still hire people?

This is not a tool upgrade.
This is cheap compute's crushing, total replacement of basic mental labor.

And the most terrifying part:
I have a master's degree, a proper formal education, years of experience, I'm still self-studying AI — and I'm already unemployed.

Then what about the vast numbers of ordinary white-collar workers with weaker foundations, lower degrees, no time to self-study, no access to information?
They have no way out at all.


150-210s Third layer of pessimism: the salary system collapses completely, ordinary people lose all pricing power

What is a wage, at its core?
The price of scarce mental labor.

Writing proposals, writing copy, planning campaigns, organizing materials —
these used to be scarce abilities that required study, experience, accumulation. That's why they commanded a salary.

Now:
top-tier model compute is priced like water and electricity
the marginal cost of standardized mental labor is roughly zero.

That directly produces two economic consequences:

1. Entry-level white-collar jobs vanish at scale (marketing planners, operations, copywriting, admin, junior analysts)
2. The remaining jobs see endless involution, salaries fall off a cliff.

The macro economy will look better and better:
GDP rising, corporate profits surging, human efficiency doubling.

But all of the dividend flows only to capital, to the compute owners, to a tiny AI elite.
Ordinary people receive not a cent of the dividend — they only bear the unemployment and the pay cuts.

This is the most deformed economic structure of our time:
technology is exploding, ordinary people's incomes are caving in.


210-265s Fourth layer, the ultimate pessimism: education is completely out of sync, and the times give ordinary people no buffer

Our education, from childhood through more than a decade of schooling:
marketing, planning, copywriting, logic, traditional workplace skills.

Not one class ever taught AI, prompts, model management, automation pipelines.

Industries used to iterate slowly; people could transform gradually.
Now models like DeepSeek iterate monthly and overturn everything by force.

Textbooks lag, schools lag, workplace training lags.
Ordinary people get no transition period, no buffer, no safety-net policy.

Everyone is forcibly required to:
pay out of pocket to retrain, self-study AI, keep up with the speed — or be eliminated.

On what grounds?
Why should ordinary workers foot the bill for technology's savage iteration, for things proper education never taught?

This is not a question of effort.
The rules were rewritten overnight, and the vast majority of people simply had no time to adapt.


265-300s Closing elevation (real, high-level, maximum resonance)

I'm not bashing AI. I admit DeepSeek V4 is powerful technology, a domestic breakthrough, a huge advance in productivity.

But I remain extremely pessimistic.

Because I have seen the most brutal economic truth:
the moment top-tier intelligence, top-tier mental compute, gets priced like cabbage, like water and electricity —
the cheapest thing in this world becomes the day-after-day mental labor of ordinary people.

In the past, technological progress let people turn their lives around.
Now, technological progress eliminates ordinary people.

Stop saying "learn AI and you'll be optimistic."
Only a minority can ever learn it.
The eliminated will always be the ordinary workers who make up the overwhelming majority of society.

The future's economic data will look beautiful.
But ordinary people's lives will only get harder, more involuted, with less and less bargaining power.

This is what DeepSeek V4 brings me: an unanswerable, utterly real economic pessimism.


Final values of the video (restrained, objective, not extreme, high-level dialectics)

1. Technology is indeed progressing; productivity is indeed exploding.
2. A tiny minority of high-end strategy, business, and decision-making roles remain valuable (but their numbers are far too few to catch the masses).
3. The vast middle layer of basic mental-labor jobs is permanently disappearing.
4. This is not a personal-capability problem; it is a structural, macro-distributional tragedy of the era.
5. Ordinary people's pessimism is completely reasonable, completely grounded in reality — not melodrama.
