## The headline isn't the hike — it's the switch

In the early hours of 2026-09-17, the Federal Reserve raised rates 25bp by a unanimous 12–0 vote, bringing the federal funds rate to 3.75%–4.00% — its first hike since July 2023. The Dow fell 1.21%, the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back above 5%, the dollar index reclaimed 100, and gold dropped sharply after the decision.

On its own, 25bp was fully priced — CME FedWatch had it above 95% before the vote. What actually repriced assets wasn't the basis points; it was **the narrative switching from "wait for the cutting cycle" back to "unwind easing, inflation first."**

## Three signals, stacked, create repricing pressure

1. **A unanimous vote**: the committee has formed an anti-inflation consensus — not a lone hawk.
2. **A raised dot plot**: the median end-2026 rate moved up from 3.8% in June to 4.1%, and 12 of 18 officials expect another hike this year. Markets are being forced to price "more this year + no easy easing in 2027."
3. **An independence fight**: the White House demanded 1%, the new chair refused — which only strengthened the hawkish credibility that "the Fed follows the data."

Taken one at a time, each can be explained away. Stacked together, they drown out the "this is just noise" reading.

## Which assets actually fear high rates

| Asset | Direction | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Treasuries | Negative (yields up) | Discount rate rises; duration reprices |
| Growth/tech | Mixed | High rates compress valuations, but the AI capex cycle is relatively independent |
| HK equities | Near-term pressure | A stronger dollar widens the valuation discount |
| Gold | Short-term negative, long-term uncertain | Rising real rates weigh on gold, but central-bank buying persists |
| RMB assets | Split market | External pressure offset by domestic credit easing |

The watershed: whether the 10-year holds above 5%, and whether the dollar index settles above 100.

## But the counterarguments are strong too

An honest analysis can't run one direction only. Three counterpoints deserve to be taken seriously:

- **Already priced in**: 25bp was fully expected; the "big reaction" looks more like technical selling and leveraged-fund repricing. If CPI rolls over, December odds collapse fast and this fades back to noise.
- **Unanimous ≠ unified — it's political**: the new chair needs a united front to cement the "independent of the White House" story. The political function of 12–0 outweighs its information content. The moment inflation softens, "unanimity" can decay into disagreement.
- **AI capex may be immune to rates**: Big Tech's AI spending is driven by competitive pressure (don't invest, get disrupted), not by the cost of capital. That means the old "high rates kill growth valuations" playbook may partly break down this cycle.

## What would falsify this call

- If October CPI falls below 2.8% year-over-year, "more hikes this year" fades fast and the narrative dies.
- If the 10-year drops below 4.5% and holds for a week, the market thinks the Fed overplayed hawkishness.
- If AI giants keep raising capex guidance in Q4 earnings, the "rate immunity" hypothesis strengthens and growth decouples from rates.

The dot plot is a median forecast, not a promise — historically about two-thirds of dot-plot paths have been contradicted by reality. That's the discount every "repricing" narrative must take.

## The takeaway

The signal in this hike isn't the 25bp; it's that "inflation first" is back in charge. It repriced duration assets and rate-sensitive valuations across the board — but whether it holds depends on the next two months of inflation data, not on a single Fed dot plot. Before going long or short, remember: what truly prices the future is data, not forecasts.
